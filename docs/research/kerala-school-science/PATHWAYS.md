# Kerala school science exhibition pathways

Research initially checked on 31 October 2026, with the administrative, archived participant-list and SISF compendium records below added on 1 November 2026. Kerala's School Sasthrolsavam, the NCERT exhibition system, Southern India Science Fair, INSPIRE-MANAK and National Children's Science Congress have different entry routes. An award in one event does not by itself establish admission to another. Rules below retain the edition or source date that supports them.

## From Sasthrolsavam to other exhibitions

KITE describes the school-to-state competition sequence and says first- and second-place state winners may participate in SISF; it also describes an opportunity for Sasthrolsavam winners to reach the national exhibition under its former Jawaharlal Nehru name. These are broad pathway descriptions. Actual participation still requires the relevant selected list, category and invitation. The page's old name and general item counts should not override a current circular. [KITE](https://mela.kite.kerala.gov.in/2026/state/index.php/result/Public_result/about_us)

NCERT explicitly renamed its national exhibition **Rashtriya Bal Vaigyanik Pradarshani from 2022**. Historical references to JNNSMEE/Jawaharlal Nehru science, mathematics and environment exhibitions therefore belong in the same archive lineage, with the original title and year preserved. [NCERT renaming notice](https://ncert.nic.in/desm/pdf/Rename_The_49th_RBVP_2022.pdf)

The Kerala circular dated 2 September 2026 is a separate source of school-fair competition topics. Its scope should be checked alongside the annual manual and item-specific instructions before assigning an NCERT theme to a Kerala entry. [Kerala topic circular](https://education.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/scincefest02092026.pdf)

## Southern India Science Fair

The official SISF 2026 instructions identify VITM and Telangana SCERT as joint organisers. They give exhibition dates of **19–23 January 2026**, with reporting on 18 January, at Gaudium International School, Kollur, RC Puram, Sangareddy, Hyderabad. These dates identify that edition, not an upcoming SISF schedule.

For that edition:

- Entry required inclusion in the respective education department's state-selected list.
- Categories were one student with an escort, two students with an escort, or a teacher presenting an exhibit.
- The escort had to belong to the student's institution and could not escort multiple exhibits or compete as a teacher exhibitor.
- Registration required identity certification, a head-of-institution letter and an A4 write-up limited to one side of one page.
- Normal display allocation was about 4 ft × 2 ft; explanation to judges was about five minutes.
- Participants had to attend throughout. Special facilities needed advance requests; dangerous, flammable or hazardous materials were excluded.

Source: [SISF 2026 instructions, PDF pages 1–2, instructions 1–16](https://www.vismuseum.gov.in/assets/pdf/notice/SISF2026_instrauctions.pdf). These are SISF conditions, not automatically Kerala state-fair conditions.

For the preceding edition, VITM records **21–25 January 2025 at Old Port Ground, Puducherry**, with separate forms for individual, group and teacher entries. This official event page is useful for reconstructing regional participation by edition. [SISF 2025](https://www.vismuseum.gov.in/events/sisf-2025.php)

## NCERT state and national exhibitions

The NCERT 2025–26 guidelines cover RSBVP 2025–26 and the 53rd national RBVP in 2026. State exhibitions can include diverse school managements, with preference for secondary/higher secondary exhibits and provision for exceptional upper-primary work. Some organisations, including KVS, NVS, CBSE independent schools and specified institutional networks, send selections directly; states should avoid duplicate forwarding.

The suggested state-stage weighting is creativity 20%; innovation 15%; scientific approach 15%; construction skills 15%; social usefulness/scalability 15%; cost/portability/durability 10%; presentation 10%. National screening uses a different weighting: innovation 25%, scientific approach 20%, usefulness/scalability 20%, practical economy 15% and written presentation 20%.

National participation requires NCERT selection after reviewing the forwarded write-up and student video, rather than merely a state prize. The documentation includes the exhibit-information proforma and an approximately five-minute video. [NCERT guidelines, printed pages 25–26 and 29, PDF pages 30–31 and 34](https://ncert.nic.in/desm/pdf/rbvp_53rd_2025.pdf)

## CBSE route for Kerala schools

CBSE circular **Acad-69/2026, 27 October 2026**, sets registration/fee submission for 27 October–14 November 2026 and regional/national events for November 2026–January 2027. It permits classes 6–8 and 9–11 in separate categories, at most two students per team with a science mentor, and up to two teams per school. The circular states a ₹3,000 registration fee, while its registration annexure calculates a fee based on team count; schools should verify the payable amount in the portal.

The circular uses the same seven broad areas as the NCERT August notification. Assessment weights are 20/15/15/15/15/10/10 for creativity, originality, science, construction, value/scalability, practical economy and presentation. Schools arrange participation expenses and parental consent. Winners are forwarded for NCERT consideration; national admission is conditional. A possible IRIS opportunity is also conditional.

Source: [CBSE circular, PDF pages 1–4 and registration annexure](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2026/69_Circular_2026.pdf). The [CBSE microsite](https://academic.cbseit.in/science_ex/) gives the regional-to-national workflow; its generic registration-month banner is less precise than the dated circular.

The [national selection evidence guide](SELECTIONS.md) and [versioned records](national-selection-index.json) add dated-edition evidence from the 2022–2024 organiser documents. The 2023 and 2024 rows distinguish Kerala state headings from KVS/NVS organisational headings; participant forms remain separate from selection and attendance. The underlying Kerala DGE nomination proceedings and recent SISF delegation lists are still missing; the separate 2014 DPI participant list above is a historical record.

## INSPIRE-MANAK

The live official programme page describes a DST/NIF route beginning with original, socially useful technological ideas. It currently allows classes **6–12**, five school nominations, and at most two ideas from science-stream classes 11/12 within that total. Selected students receive ₹10,000 through DBT. The stated national selection funnel is 100,000 initial awards, 10,000 state-stage ideas, 1,000 national exhibits and 60 leading innovations. Novelty, usefulness, environmental impact, ease of use and advantage over existing approaches inform selection. These are programme targets, not Kerala participation totals. [INSPIRE programme page](https://www.inspireawards-dst.gov.in/UserP/Award.aspx)

The FAQ specifies individual rather than group nomination and accepts ideas in scheduled Indian languages. It asks for a meaningful project title and a synopsis describing the problem, proposed solution and advantage. Existing pictures/sketches can help reviewers. It discourages copied textbook displays, purchased sensor kits and familiar demonstrations without original improvement. The same FAQ still contains older two-to-three-nomination wording, conflicting with the five-nomination programme page. Its generic month-by-month calendar should not substitute for a year's notification. [INSPIRE FAQ](https://www.inspireawards-dst.gov.in/UserP/faqs.aspx)

The signed **13 July 2026 notification**, now retrieved, confirms classes VI–XII and up to five original ideas per recognised school per financial year for 2026–27. Its initial nomination window was **15 July–15 September 2026**. A later [homepage observation](INSPIRE_MODELS.md#current-nomination-notice-and-later-homepage-wording) announces an extension to **10 October 2026**, alongside the current closed status; its linked PDF remains byte-identical to this July notice. No separate signed extension order was recovered. It resolves the quota conflict with older FAQ wording. The separate XI/XII science-stream condition appears on the live About page, not in this notification. [DST notification](https://inspireawards-dst.gov.in/Download/Nomination_Open_Inspire_Manak_2026-27.pdf)

For historical comparison, CBSE circular Acad-110/2022 of **22 September 2022** specified ages 10–15 and classes 6–10. That historical boundary must not be applied unchanged to the current cycle. The live portal says FY2026–27 nominations are closed as observed on 31 October. [2022–23 circular](https://www.inspireawards-dst.gov.in/download/news/CBSE_Circular_2022.pdf), [official communications index](https://www.inspireawards-dst.gov.in/UserP/viewImpComm.aspx)

The separate [INSPIRE compendium guide](INSPIRE_MODELS.md) adds national-project summaries, a public volume-link inventory and current-event/nomination date distinctions. These entries do not increase the NCERT/SISF method counts or establish a Kerala Sasthrolsavam qualifying route.

## National Children's Science Congress

KSCSTE's Kerala page, updated **18 July 2026**, identifies the council as state nodal agency. Its usual team is two children in the same age group. General groups are ages 10–14 and above 14–17, calculated on 31 December of the participation year. A scientifically capable adult may guide the work; the guide need not be a schoolteacher. The page provides a separate provision for children with disabilities, a 2026 district/academic-coordinator directory and the national registration portal. Its wording about disability grouping should be checked with the coordinator when applying to a particular child. [KSCSTE NCSC](https://kscste.kerala.gov.in/national-childrens-science-congress-ncsc/)

The Kerala-linked **2026–27 activity guidebook** gives a sustainability theme with five areas: waste reduction/recovery, energy investigation/improvement, water management, food/agriculture/health, and sustainability applications of Indian knowledge. It supplies research examples and teaching guidance. These are starter investigations, not a list of winning projects.

For documentation it stresses dated, real-time handwritten observations, rather than a rewritten fair copy or printed raw data. It suggests a 150–250-word abstract and a structured report covering the question, previous work, methods, evidence, interpretation, conclusions, sources and acknowledgements. The guide gives both a short practice explanation and an illustrative longer presentation duration; these teaching suggestions do not establish the actual time limit at a Kerala congress. [Activity guidebook, printed pages 9–12 and 52–57, PDF pages 13–16 and 56–61](https://kscste.kerala.gov.in/storage/2025/10/Activity-Guidebook-for-NCSC-2026-27_0.pdf)

The national programme site's judging guidance emphasises the child's own idea, theme fit, scientific understanding, data, analysis, validation, teamwork and communication. It calls for an authenticated daily log, with follow-up and improvement evidence relevant at later stages. This research process differs from simply displaying an attractive model. The undated page should be read with the edition's evaluation forms before quoting exact marks. [NCSC judging guidance](https://ncsc.co.in/judgement)
