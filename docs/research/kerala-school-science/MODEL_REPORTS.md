## Distinguish construction, proposals and scientific validation

The trolley's automatic sensor-triggered arm is explicitly a future improvement. The ambulance chapter describes a circuit model and broader proposed uses; it does not show an AI system or an operational emergency-service network. The plant chapters describe concept displays rather than measured power plants.

The 2013 solar chapter prints output expressions such as `MV/day` and `GV`. These are retained as unverified source wording, never silently converted into validated `MW` figures. Some plant-process and by-product descriptions also need separate scientific verification. An NCERT publication establishes what the credited report says; it does not make every student assertion a scientifically verified result.

The 2011 crane chapter includes a “no loss of energy” claim without supporting tests. Its mechanical-advantage calculation and signal-frequency wording also need independent checking; listed 6 V motor materials and described 9 V operation require clarification. Its circuits establish a described remote-control design, without establishing AI, source-code authorship or a safe operational crane.

For Karen's preparation, the useful pattern is a clear problem, component roles, actual construction, operation, test observations and honest limitations. These historical reports do not replace Karen's own construction record or establish current competition eligibility. A proposed capability should remain labelled as a proposal in charts, speech and any future knowledge answers.
