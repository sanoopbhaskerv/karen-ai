# Kerala school fair rules and participation evidence

Research date: 31 October 2026. Rules below retain their document dates and event streams. A competition's official entry name, category and item code determine which rules apply.

## Karen's current target is Science Working Model

The latest clarified target is the **2026 subdistrict Science Fair Working Model**. For a class-9 pupil this is the HS section, item **115**. The official catalogue records a group item with maximum capacity two; the manual itself describes two pupils per HS Working Model entry and requires the stipulated group-member count to attend. A school's one winning entry and the number of pupils in that entry are different quantities.

The relevant wording is at [printed pages 40–41, PDF pages 51–52](https://mela.kite.kerala.gov.in/2025/entry_forms/sasthrolsavam_manual_2025.pdf#page=51): the Working Model description ends with “രണ്ട് കുട്ടികൾ വീതം” (two pupils each). [Printed page 44, PDF page 55, note 3](https://mela.kite.kerala.gov.in/2025/entry_forms/sasthrolsavam_manual_2025.pdf#page=55) requires the designated participant count in group items to be present. Confirm any proposed single-pupil exception against the saved school entry or an explicit organiser clarification; the catalogue's word maximum alone does not establish such an exception.

A prepared Science Working Model, the IT fair's on-the-spot computer tasks, and Science Electronics/IoT/Robotics on-the-spot construction are separate events. Karen's use of a microcontroller or AI does not by itself change its registered stream or make the digital-event restrictions applicable. The prepared Working Model requirements and scoring weights are set out below; the IT comparison remains for reference because that alternative was considered during entry clarification.

## General school categories and advancement

The [2025 manual, printed pages 1 and 3–5, PDF pages 12 and 14–16](https://mela.kite.kerala.gov.in/2025/entry_forms/sasthrolsavam_manual_2025.pdf#page=14) covers government, aided and recognised unaided schools under the General Education Department.

| Category | Classes | Student progression stated in the general rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I / LP | 1–4 | School and subdistrict; finishes at subdistrict |
| II / UP | 5–7 | School and subdistrict; finishes at subdistrict |
| III / HS | 8–10 | Through revenue district and state |
| IV / HSS/VHSS | 11–12 | Through revenue district and state |

School winners with first place and A grade advance to subdistrict. For HS/HSS, first and second place with A grade at subdistrict and revenue district advance to the next level. **Science drama is an explicit exception:** its science chapter and 2026 circular advance first place only within Kerala. The general participation rule permits one non-quiz item in one fair and, additionally, a quiz in a fair; consult the school for the applicable registration combination.

Teacher events are separate from pupils' LP/UP progression. Science teaching/learning aid and teacher project contests have primary, HS and HSS/VHSS teacher categories and run through the state level. The relevant provisions are [printed pages 60–62, PDF pages 71–73](https://mela.kite.kerala.gov.in/2025/entry_forms/sasthrolsavam_manual_2025.pdf#page=71). Do not infer a state-level UP pupil competition from a portal label covering primary teachers.

### Research and investigation presentation

Research-type and investigatory projects have six minutes for presentation and four for interaction; evaluate the report separately. Construction/research participation evidence may include photographs and a video of at most five minutes. The report is submitted on registration day. See [manual printed pages 39–43, 50–51 and 57–58](https://mela.kite.kerala.gov.in/2025/entry_forms/sasthrolsavam_manual_2025.pdf#page=50).

The [2 September 2026 Kerala circular, PDF pages 1–3](https://education.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/scincefest02092026.pdf), explicitly adopts the attached NCERT letter of 28 August 2026 for science fairs and science seminars from school to state.

2. Green Energy and Environmental Conservation.
3. Mathematical Modelling and Computational Thinking.
4. Health, Hygiene, and Sanitation.
5. Indigenous Agricultural Practices.
6. Science for Rural Transformation.

The Kerala cover permits other subthemes related to the main theme. Seminar topics are STEM for Climate Action, and Non-Communicable Diseases: Causes and Preventive Measures.

These science themes do not replace the IT presentation theme in the KITE circular.

## Science Electronics, IoT and Robotics in 2026

The [31 August 2026 DGE circular, PDF pages 1–2](https://education.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/sasthrolsavam31082026.pdf#page=1), applies to HS/HSS/VHSE on-the-spot events. Assembly and demonstration finish within three hours; a design document is prepared during the first half-hour. Prebuilt or fully presoldered complete systems are excluded, while submodules/components are allowed. Pupils must explain the hardware, software and code; unmodified plug-and-play kits are excluded.

The circular distinguishes engineering design and problem solving from merely assembling parts. IoT entries demonstrate sensors, a microcontroller, communication, real-time data and the data flow; an app without hardware is insufficient. Robotics must include sensing, processing, action and automation; remote control alone is insufficient. It also requires compliance with safety rules. These provisions should not be applied to a different event without an explicit cross-reference.

## Science magazine update

The [same 2026 circular, PDF pages 2–3](https://education.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/sasthrolsavam31082026.pdf#page=2), describes a five-pupil school magazine committee at subdistrict, then two A-grade high-scoring committee members progressing at each subsequent level. Confirm nomination mechanics locally; do not assume the catalogue's five-person capacity persists unchanged at district/state.

It describes a two-day workshop: two hours for reference, three hours of magazine preparation on the first day and two on the second, followed by interaction. Notes made during reference cannot be used during magazine production. The handwritten, bound magazine is limited to 30 pages including the cover, uses one side of A4 sheets, and omits school/subdistrict/district identifiers. Magazine results do not count toward the fair championship. Use the circular for complete binding and production directions.

### Current conduct and software

The [3 September 2026 KITE circular, page 1](https://kite.kerala.gov.in/KITE/itsadmin/uploads/docs/1136.pdf#page=1) specifies KITE Ubuntu 22.04 for IT contests. Its operational changes are:

- Malayalam typing uses the default Poorna/Extended Inscript layout. Applications may be used for typing chillu characters.
- Animation may use OpenToonz as well as the previously permitted applications; save the export in MP4/AVI.
- Composition and presentation has a five-minute presentation and the theme **Digital Horizon**.
- For webpage design, select suitable supplied content and modify it as needed.
- Scratch programming uses Scratch 3/TurboWarp.

The manual's [printed page 166, PDF page 177](https://mela.kite.kerala.gov.in/2025/entry_forms/sasthrolsavam_manual_2025.pdf#page=177) states that IT contests are conducted on the spot, organisers provide computers, and computers are allotted by drawing lots. It excludes externally prepared material and personal digital gadgets, including storage devices and mobile phones, from the contest venue.

These IT sources do **not** provide the rubric for Karen's prepared Science Working Model. Their restrictions on externally prepared material and personal gadgets should not be transferred to Science Working Model without an explicit applicable rule. No student IT Working Model event was found in the inspected statewide documents.

## Examination grace marks are separate from fair points

For **2025–26 examinations only**, the [16 March 2026 circular, PDF pages 1–3 and 5–6](https://education.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/grace-mark-2025-26_circular-16.03.2026.pdf) records state Sasthrolsavam grades A/B/C as 20/15/10 grace marks. For ranked items it records first/second/third as 20/17/14. Southern India Science Fair first-three-place winners receive 22; the national exhibition entry listed in that circular receives 25.

The circular contains conditions for earlier class-8/9 achievements, class-10 district A grades, highest eligible activity rather than addition across activities, and online applications. Its March 2026 submission deadlines are historical. Do not promise a 2026–27 participant these marks without that examination cycle's orders.
